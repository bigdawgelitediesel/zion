# 250 Reasons to Move from Utah to Cheyenne, Wyoming

Written 2026-10-20. Aimed at people living along the Wasatch Front who are feeling the squeeze: housing, traffic, air, taxes, water, crowds. Every reason below is a real, checkable difference or a real Cheyenne asset. Dollar figures are approximate and current as of fall 2026.

## Taxes and your paycheck

1. Wyoming has no state income tax. Utah takes a flat cut of roughly 4.5% off every dollar you earn. On a $100k household that is about $4,500 a year back in your pocket, every year, forever.
2. No state tax on capital gains. Sell stock, sell a business, sell crypto, sell a rental, and Wyoming takes nothing.
3. No state tax on Social Security. Utah taxes it once your income clears its credit thresholds.
4. No state tax on pensions, 401(k) or IRA withdrawals, or military retirement. Retirement income is simply untaxed here.
5. No state tax on rental income, side hustles, or your spouse's business. Every income stream is state-tax-free.
6. Remote workers keep more. If you earn a Denver, California, or East Coast salary from a home office, Wyoming's zero rate applies.
7. Groceries are exempt from sales tax in Wyoming. Utah still taxes food.
8. Lower sales tax overall. Cheyenne's combined rate is 6%. Most Wasatch Front cities run above 7%.
9. Wyoming has no corporate income tax. Utah charges one.
10. No franchise tax, no gross receipts tax, no inventory tax. The Tax Foundation has ranked Wyoming's business tax climate #1 in the nation for over a decade.
11. No estate tax and no inheritance tax. What you built passes to your kids intact.
12. Property taxes are among the lowest in the country, and the 2025 Legislature cut the tax on owner-occupied homes by 25% on top of that.
13. Long-time homeowners 65 and up who have paid Wyoming property taxes for 25 years get a 50% exemption.
14. Veterans get a property tax exemption on their home.
15. Mineral wealth pays the bills. Coal, oil, gas, and trona royalties fund a large share of the state budget, which is exactly why residents are taxed so lightly.
16. Wyoming's Permanent Mineral Trust Fund is a multi-billion-dollar endowment that throws off income for the state every year.
17. Wyoming's constitution limits state debt. No future tax hike is waiting to pay off bonds.
18. Wyoming's gas tax is 24 cents a gallon. Utah's is about 38.
19. No toll roads anywhere in Wyoming. Utah's I-15 express lanes charge you to skip the traffic you would otherwise be stuck in.
20. No vehicle emissions testing and no annual safety inspection anywhere in Wyoming. Five Wasatch Front counties require emissions testing.
21. Car insurance runs cheaper here. Fewer cars, fewer claims, lower premiums.
22. No state income tax return to file. April gets simpler and your take-home shows up on paycheck one.
23. Wyoming invented the LLC in 1977 and still has some of the strongest LLC privacy and asset-protection laws in the country.
24. Wyoming dynasty trusts can run up to 1,000 years. The state is a top-tier trust jurisdiction, which is why wealthy families park assets here.
25. Wyoming is the most crypto- and blockchain-friendly state in the union, with legal recognition for DAOs and special-purpose depository institutions.
26. Business filings are cheap and fast. A Wyoming LLC costs about $100 to form and about $60 a year to maintain.
27. Lower cost of living overall. Cheyenne runs below the national average while Salt Lake runs above it.
28. Wyoming ranks at or near the bottom nationally for total state and local tax burden on residents. Utah is middle of the pack.

## Housing and land

29. Median home price in Cheyenne is roughly $390k. Salt Lake County single-family homes hit a record $645k in mid-2026.
30. Salt Lake County homebuyers now need roughly $186k in household income to afford a median home. Cheyenne needs far less, and Utah ranks among the least affordable housing markets in the country relative to income.
31. New construction is plentiful. Cheyenne is adding subdivisions on the north and east sides, not fighting over the last lot in a canyon.
32. Acreage is 10 minutes from downtown. Five- and ten-acre parcels with horses are a normal way to live in Laramie County.
33. No HOA if you don't want one. Plenty of established neighborhoods and most county parcels have none.
34. You can keep horses, chickens, goats, a shop, and a camper on your property without a fight.
35. Room for the big shop building. Detached shops and pole barns are standard out here.
36. Bigger lots in town, too. Cheyenne's older neighborhoods have real yards.
37. Historic homes. The cattle-baron mansions and Victorian streets around 17th Street and the Capitol are affordable compared to the Avenues in Salt Lake.
38. Rent is cheaper. A one-bedroom in Cheyenne runs several hundred dollars a month less than the Salt Lake market.
39. Lower closing costs. No state real estate transfer tax, and title and recording fees are small-county cheap.
40. Cheyenne isn't crammed against mountains. The city has room to grow in every direction, which keeps land prices honest.
41. Dark skies 15 minutes from town. Milky Way from the backyard.
42. No wildland-urban interface problem. Wasatch foothill homes are getting hard to insure. Cheyenne's prairie is not.
43. Property tax on a $400k house runs about $2,000 a year after the 2025 cut.
44. Building permits and inspections are handled by a city of 65,000, not a metro of 1.2 million. Things move.
45. Sell a $600k house in Utah, buy a nicer one in Cheyenne for $400k, and bank the difference with no state capital gains tax.
46. Land kept in agricultural production is taxed on what it produces, not what it would sell for. A parcel running cattle or hay pays a fraction of market-value property tax.
47. Domestic and stock wells are permitted with a simple State Engineer form. No water-rights fight to water a house and a few head.
48. No HOA means the trailer, the camper, and the project truck live in your own driveway.
49. Hay is grown all around Cheyenne and Wheatland. You buy it by the ton from a neighbor, not by the bale from a store.
50. The Laramie County Conservation District sells windbreak seedlings cheap every spring, and the UW Extension office in town answers ag questions for free.
51. Horse country for real. Your own pasture costs nothing to board on, and arenas and trails are everywhere.

## Jobs and the economy

52. F.E. Warren Air Force Base is first in line for the Sentinel ICBM program, a multi-decade modernization bringing thousands of construction and support jobs.
53. Cheyenne is a data center hub. 10 data center campuses operating, 5 under construction, and 9 more in planning as of mid-2026.
54. Microsoft announced plans in 2026 to buy about 3,200 more acres in Cheyenne for data center expansion, on top of what it already runs.
55. Meta operates a major data center campus here too.
56. State government is headquartered here. Stable jobs with Wyoming retirement benefits.
57. Union Pacific has been Cheyenne's backbone since 1867 and is still a major employer.
58. Walmart and Lowe's both run regional distribution centers in Cheyenne.
59. Taco John's was born in Cheyenne in 1969 and five stores are still here. Yes, the Potato Olés people. (The corporate office moved to Minneapolis in 2025.)
60. Cheyenne Regional Medical Center is the region's major hospital and one of the biggest employers in town.
61. The Cheyenne VA Medical Center is here, with VA jobs and VA care.
62. Skilled trades are in high demand. Electricians, HVAC, diesel techs, and lineworkers get hired fast.
63. Diesel and heavy-truck work is a real industry here. I-80 is one of the busiest freight corridors in America, and it runs right through town.
64. Unemployment in Laramie County consistently runs low. Employers compete for workers, not the other way around.
65. A wage earned here is worth more. No income tax plus cheaper housing is a raise without changing jobs.
66. Denver's job market is 90 minutes away. Plenty of people live in Cheyenne and work hybrid in Colorado.
67. Wyoming's professional licensing boards are small and responsive. Transferring a license is manageable.
68. Starting a business is cheap, lightly regulated, and welcomed. The state wants you here.
69. Agriculture is still real here. Ranching, hay, and cattle are an economy, not a museum.
70. Energy jobs. Wyoming is the nation's top coal producer and a top-ten oil and gas state, and Cheyenne is the capital where that industry does business.
71. Wind energy is booming on the plains around Cheyenne, with tech and maintenance jobs to match.
72. Trucking and logistics. The I-80 and I-25 interchange makes Cheyenne a natural distribution point for the whole Mountain West.
73. Wyoming's Food Freedom Act lets you sell homemade food, from sourdough to jam to eggs, straight to neighbors with no commercial kitchen and no inspection. It's the broadest law of its kind in the country, and it's exactly what a family bakery and farm stand need.
74. Under the same law, families can sell beef by animal share: the customer buys a share of the animal and takes home the meat. That's how a small herd turns into freezer beef for the neighbors.
75. Raw milk, farm eggs, and garden produce can all go straight from the place to the customer. No middleman, no license.
76. The Cheyenne Farmers Market runs Saturdays at the Depot Plaza from August into October, and the Front Range markets in Fort Collins and Denver are 45 to 90 minutes. That's a customer base of three million people for whatever the family makes.
77. Torrington Livestock, one of the biggest sale barns in the region, is 90 minutes away.
78. Millions of travelers a year roll past on I-80 and I-25. A farm stand or a sign at the gate has customers without advertising.

## Schools and college

79. Wyoming spends roughly $20,500 per student. Utah spends about $11,300, dead last or close to it nationally.
80. Smaller class sizes. Utah has some of the largest class sizes in the country. Wyoming does not.
81. Wyoming funds schools from state mineral revenue, so your property tax isn't the thing standing between your kid and a new gym.
82. Laramie County School District 1 is the largest district in Wyoming, with the course offerings and sports programs to match.
83. The Hathaway Scholarship. Wyoming high school grads earn a state-funded scholarship to UW or any Wyoming community college based on GPA and ACT. It covers a big chunk of tuition.
84. University of Wyoming in-state tuition is among the lowest in the nation for a flagship research university.
85. UW is 45 minutes away in Laramie. Your kid can live at home freshman year if they want, or come home for Sunday dinner.
86. Laramie County Community College is in town, with transfer pathways to UW and strong trades programs.
87. LCCC's diesel, welding, and wind-energy programs feed directly into local employers.
88. Wyoming's school facilities are new or recently rebuilt. The state funded a massive capital construction program in the 2000s and 2010s.
89. Every Wyoming school district gets equalized funding. A kid in Cheyenne gets the same per-pupil money as a kid in Jackson.
90. Wyoming has one of the best school-nurse-to-student ratios in the country. Utah has one of the worst.
91. Strong career-and-technical education at the high school level, including shop classes that still exist.
92. School sports are big and accessible. In a state of 580,000 people, your kid can actually make the team.
93. No lottery schools, no decade-long charter waitlists. Open enrollment within the district is straightforward.
94. Wyoming's homeschool law is light. An annual notice to the district, no state testing. For a big family that wants the option, it's there.

## Air, water, weather, and natural risk

95. No winter inversions. Cheyenne sits on open plains at 6,062 feet with no bowl to trap smog, so the January air that ranks among the worst in the nation in Salt Lake is clean here.
96. No Great Salt Lake dust. The lake is shrinking and its exposed bed carries arsenic-laden dust into the Wasatch Front. That problem doesn't move with you.
97. No Wasatch Fault. Geologists put real odds on a major earthquake hitting the Wasatch Front in your lifetime. Cheyenne's seismic risk is close to nil.
98. Earthquake insurance is a line item you can delete.
99. Cooler summers. Cheyenne's average July high is in the low 80s. Salt Lake's is in the low 90s with 100-degree stretches.
100. Summer nights drop into the 50s. Open the windows and skip the AC.
101. The wind is real, but it's also why the air is clean, the snow melts, and the mosquitoes can't fly.
102. Sunshine most days of the year, even in winter.
103. Snow doesn't pile up. Sun and wind clear a six-inch storm off the roads in a day or two.
104. Low humidity year-round. No sticky summer.
105. Water is reliable. Cheyenne pulls from the Snowy Range watershed via pipelines plus local wells, and the Board of Public Utilities plans decades ahead.
106. No lawn-watering lottery. Utah's drought restrictions get tighter every year.
107. Utah is the second-driest state and its water fights are about to get ugly. Wyoming is the headwaters state.
108. Wildfire smoke affects the whole West, but Cheyenne has no canyon-mouth neighborhoods waiting to burn.
109. No Lake Powell or Great Salt Lake crisis in your backyard, politically or literally.
110. Winter driving is better. Fewer cars, dry pavement most days, and no Parley's Canyon.
111. You'll live acclimated to 6,000 feet, so every hunt, hike, and ski day in the high country starts easier.
112. Sunsets run the full width of the sky out here. No mountain in the way.

## Traffic, commute, and daily life

113. Average commute in Cheyenne is about 15 minutes. Across town is 15 minutes. Across town at rush hour is still 15 minutes.
114. No I-15. The Wasatch Front's main artery is a parking lot from Lehi to Ogden twice a day.
115. No Point of the Mountain, no Bangerter, no Legacy, no express lanes. Cheyenne is a grid you learn in a week.
116. No red-air-day wood-burning bans. Utah restricts fireplaces and stoves on inversion days. Cheyenne has no inversions and no ban.
117. Free parking downtown. Free parking everywhere, really.
118. The hospital, the airport, Sam's Club, and your kid's school are all within 10 minutes of each other.
119. A 65,000-person city with every national chain you actually need (Target, Home Depot, Lowe's, Menards, Sam's, Walmart) and none of the sprawl.
120. Two interstates intersect here. Anywhere in the West is a straight shot.
121. Cheyenne Regional Airport has commercial service to Denver's United hub, 20 minutes from your house.
122. Denver International, with nonstops worldwide, is about an hour and 45 minutes door to door with no traffic surprises.
123. Fewer people everywhere. Shorter lines at the DMV, the doctor, the grocery store, the trailhead.
124. Utah grew faster than almost any state in the 2010s and it shows. Cheyenne is growing at a pace you can keep up with.
125. Local government is reachable. The mayor and city council answer email and show up at the diner.
126. The Laramie County Library was Library Journal's 2008 Library of the Year. It's a stunning building and a real community hub.
127. Parking at Cheyenne Regional Airport is free.
128. Groceries are covered: King Soopers, Albertsons, Safeway, two Walmarts, Sam's Club, and Natural Grocers, all within 15 minutes of each other.
129. Costco, Trader Joe's, and Whole Foods are 45 minutes away in Fort Collins, closer than Costco is to half of Utah County at 5 p.m.

## Outdoors and recreation

130. Curt Gowdy State Park is 25 minutes west. IMBA Epic-rated mountain bike trails, three reservoirs, climbing, camping, and an archery range.
131. Vedauwoo is 30 minutes away. World-famous granite crack climbing and bouldering, and some of the best picnic spots in the West.
132. Medicine Bow National Forest starts 20 minutes from town along Happy Jack Road. Hiking, hunting, dispersed camping, snowmobiling.
133. The Snowy Range is 2 hours away. 12,000-foot peaks, alpine lakes, and Medicine Bow Peak as a day hike.
134. Snowy Range Ski Area sells lift tickets for a fraction of what Snowbird or Park City charge. Short lines, real snow.
135. Colorado skiing is close. Winter Park and Eldora are about 2.5 hours, Steamboat about 3.5.
136. With the money you save on taxes and housing, you can fly back and ski Utah's best powder days as a tourist and still come out ahead.
137. Rocky Mountain National Park is 2 hours away.
138. Cheyenne's Greenway is 40-plus miles of paved trail through town for biking, running, and walking.
139. Lions Park and Sloan's Lake. Paddle boats, fishing, disc golf, the Botanic Gardens, and the city's big summer festivals.
140. Cheyenne Botanic Gardens is free, with a conservatory and the Paul Smith Children's Village.
141. Holliday Park has one of the few surviving Big Boy steam locomotives (No. 4004) sitting out in the open where kids can touch it.
142. The Cheyenne Aquatic Center is an indoor water park and lap pool open year-round.
143. Ice hockey and skating at the Cheyenne Ice & Events Center.
144. Golf is cheap. Several public courses in town and no $150 green fees.
145. Pole Mountain's Blair-Wallis area has forested trails 20 minutes from downtown that feel like the Uintas without the drive.
146. Granite, Crystal, and North Crow reservoirs for kayaking, paddleboarding, and trout 30 minutes out.
147. Terry Bison Ranch south of town. Ride a train through a bison herd, then eat a bison burger.
148. Glendo and Guernsey reservoirs are 1.5 to 2 hours north for boating and wakeboarding.
149. Snowmobiling in the Snowy Range and the Sierra Madre is world-class and not crowded.
150. ATV and UTV trails on national forest land are open and plentiful.
151. Dirt bikes, side-by-sides, and trucks are a normal part of life. Nobody flinches at a lifted diesel.
152. Shooting ranges in town and open BLM and state land where you can shoot legally and safely.
153. Horse country. Boarding, arenas, trails, and a culture that still rides.
154. Rodeo every week in summer. Hell on Wheels Rodeo and the Frontier Days complex keep the arena busy.
155. Cross-country skiing and snowshoeing at Happy Jack and the Snowy Range on groomed trails.
156. The Cheyenne Frontier Days air show, often featuring the Thunderbirds, every July over your own town.
157. Pronghorn, elk, and mule deer are visible on a normal drive to Laramie.
158. Dog parks, dog-friendly trails, and room for a working dog to actually work.
159. No timed-entry reservations and no lotteries. Utah's Arches and Angels Landing need a permit months out; Wyoming's parks and forests mostly just need you to show up.
160. Campgrounds that still have open sites on a Friday afternoon.
161. A Wyoming resident state park pass is about $40 for the year, good at every park in the state.

## Hunting and fishing

162. Wyoming residents can buy a general elk license over the counter. Utah's draws are brutal.
163. Resident big-game tags are cheap. Elk, deer, and antelope licenses each run well under $100.
164. Wyoming has more pronghorn than anywhere on earth. Antelope season is practically a fall holiday.
165. Resident preference points and draw odds are far better than Utah's for limited-quota hunts.
166. Mule deer, elk, antelope, bighorn, moose, mountain goat, bison, black bear, and mountain lion all live here.
167. Upland birds. Pheasant, sharptail, and sage grouse, plus the Springer wildlife area northeast of town.
168. Waterfowl along the Platte and the eastern plains reservoirs.
169. Wyoming's Walk-In Areas and Hunter Management Areas open hundreds of thousands of private acres to residents for free.
170. A resident fishing license with the conservation stamp is under $50 a year.
171. The North Platte's Miracle Mile and Grey Reef, two of the best trout fisheries in America, are under 3 hours away.
172. The Laramie Plains lakes (Hattie, Twin Buttes, Meeboer) hold huge rainbows 1 hour west.
173. Ice fishing is a real winter sport here.
174. Game and Fish is funded, staffed, and actually answers the phone.
175. Taxidermists, meat processors, and gun shops are everywhere because the culture is everywhere.
176. You can hunt in the morning and be at your desk by 9.
177. Resident youth big-game licenses cost a fraction of adult prices, so every kid in the family can hunt.

## Location and proximity

178. Denver is 90 minutes down I-25. Big-city everything when you want it, none of it when you don't.
179. Fort Collins is 45 minutes. Colorado State, a huge brewery scene, Old Town, and Costco.
180. Laramie is 45 minutes. University of Wyoming, Cowboys football at the highest stadium in Division I, and a classic college town.
181. Denver Broncos, Nuggets, Avalanche, Rockies, and Rapids are all 90 minutes away.
182. Ball Arena, Empower Field, Coors Field, and Red Rocks. Every major concert tour comes within reach.
183. Children's Hospital Colorado and UCHealth's Anschutz campus, some of the best specialty medicine in the country, are 90 minutes away for the rare thing Cheyenne can't handle.
184. UCHealth's Poudre Valley Hospital in Fort Collins is 45 minutes for a second option.
185. Salt Lake City is a 6.5-hour straight shot west on I-80. Family is a day's drive, not a flight.
186. Same time zone as Utah. No math when you call Mom.
187. Yellowstone and Grand Teton are in your state. Residents treat them as a long weekend, not a bucket-list trip.
188. Devils Tower, Thermopolis hot springs, Fort Laramie, Independence Rock, and the Oregon Trail ruts are all day trips.
189. The Black Hills and Mount Rushmore are about 5 hours.
190. Nebraska's Lake McConaughy is 3 hours for a different kind of lake weekend.
191. Western Nebraska's Scotts Bluff and Chimney Rock are 90 minutes for a history fix.
192. You're 20 minutes from Colorado and pay Wyoming's taxes.
193. Pine Bluffs' Our Lady of Peace Shrine, the Ames Monument, Tree Rock, and the Lincoln bust at the I-80 summit are the kind of roadside stops that make the drive fun.
194. Pine Bluffs, Burns, and Carpenter are 20 to 30 minutes east with their own small schools, if you want even smaller than Cheyenne.

## Culture, events, food, and drink

195. Cheyenne Frontier Days, "the Daddy of 'em All," is the world's largest outdoor rodeo and has run every year since 1897.
196. Ten days of rodeo, four parades, three free pancake breakfasts that serve 100,000 pancakes to 15,000-plus people, a carnival, an Indian Village, and nightly concerts from the biggest acts in country music.
197. Cheyenne Day. The Wednesday of Frontier Days, half the town closes and goes to the rodeo.
198. Frontier Days runs on 2,500 volunteers. Join a committee and you're part of the town within a year.
199. Union Pacific's Steam Shop is in Cheyenne. Big Boy 4014, the largest operating steam locomotive on earth, lives here and rolls out for excursions.
200. The restored 1886 Union Pacific Depot anchors downtown, with a museum, plaza, and the summer concert series.
201. Fridays on the Plaza. Free live concerts at the Depot every Friday night all summer.
202. The Wyoming State Capitol was fully restored in 2019 and is free to tour. You'll see your legislators in the halls.
203. Wyoming State Museum. Free admission.
204. Cheyenne Frontier Days Old West Museum has one of the best Western carriage collections anywhere.
205. Nelson Museum of the West, Cowgirls of the West Museum, Messenger's Old West Museum, and the Historic Governors' Mansion. A lot of history for a city this size.
206. Cheyenne Little Theatre Players has run continuously since 1930, one of the oldest community theatres in America.
207. Cheyenne Symphony Orchestra and touring Broadway shows at the Cheyenne Civic Center.
208. Cheyenne Gunslingers put on free Old West shootout shows downtown every summer.
209. A half dozen local breweries, Freedom's Edge and Accomplice among them.
210. Wyoming Brewers Festival every June at the Depot.
211. Normal liquor laws. Private liquor stores, wine and spirits at the grocery-attached store, full-strength beer in grocery aisles, no state-run liquor stores.
212. Normal bar rules. Happy hour exists, you can order a drink without ordering food, and liquor stores are open Sundays.
213. The Albany, a downtown institution since 1942, for a steak under the old stained glass.
214. Prime rib at Terry Bison Ranch, burgers at 2 Doors Down, breakfast at the Bread Basket. The food scene punches above its weight.
215. Cheyenne Celtic Festival, the Christmas Parade, Superday in Lions Park, and a packed summer calendar.
216. Cheyenne's downtown has had a decade of real investment. New hotels, restaurants, the Depot Plaza, and The Lincoln as a live music venue.
217. "Code of the West" is literally Wyoming's official state code. Live each day with courage, take pride in your work, ride for the brand. It's the local ethic.
218. Rodeo, ranching, and railroads aren't themed attractions here. They're the neighbors.
219. One area code. 307 is an identity, not a phone prefix.
220. The bucking horse and rider on the license plate has been there since 1936. People get it tattooed.
221. The Cowboy State is also the Equality State. First to let women vote (1869), first woman governor (1925), first woman justice of the peace. Independent on its own terms.
222. The Laramie County Fair every August is free to walk into, and it's where our kids would show their steers, lambs, and chickens.

## Laws, freedom, and government

223. Constitutional carry. No permit needed to carry a firearm, open or concealed.
224. In 2025 Wyoming repealed most of its gun-free zones. The state trusts you.
225. Fireworks are legal to buy in Laramie County year-round and legal to shoot outside city limits.
226. Wyoming's legislature is a citizen legislature that meets 20 days in even years and 40 in odd years. They go home and leave you alone.
227. With about 580,000 people and two U.S. senators, a Wyoming vote carries more weight in Washington than anywhere else in the country.
228. Your state legislator is a rancher, a nurse, or a small-business owner you'll see at the hardware store. Access is real.
229. Property rights are taken seriously. Water rights, mineral rights, and grazing rights are a working legal tradition here.
230. Wyoming's sunshine laws keep local government open. Public records requests get answered.
231. Low regulatory burden on small business. Licensing, permitting, and zoning are handled at human scale.
232. No state restrictions on wood stoves, gas appliances, or generators on your own property.
233. A culture of leaving people alone. Wyoming's politics are conservative, but the day-to-day ethic is live and let live.
234. Nobody is going to ask what ward you're in, or whether you go at all. For those who do, there are strong wards and stakes in Cheyenne and the Fort Collins Temple is about 50 minutes away. For those who don't, it never comes up.
235. Religious and cultural diversity at small-town scale. Catholic, Lutheran, Baptist, LDS, Orthodox, and none-of-the-above all share the same coffee shops.
236. Side-by-sides and UTVs can be plated as multipurpose vehicles and driven on most roads except the interstate. You can drive the Ranger to the neighbor's.
237. The Legislature meets a few blocks from downtown and the galleries are open. Your kids can watch the Wyoming Senate on a school day.

## Family, health, and community

238. F.E. Warren's commissary, exchange, and clinic serve the large military and retiree community.
239. Wyoming's military retiree benefits stack. No tax on retirement pay, a property tax exemption, and a base in town.
240. Childcare and youth sports are cheaper and less waitlisted than along the Wasatch Front.
241. Laramie County 4-H and FFA are huge. Kids raise steers, show lambs, and learn to weld.
242. The YMCA, Boys & Girls Club, and city rec programs are affordable and accessible.
243. Big families fit here. Big houses, big yards, big trucks, and nobody blinks at six kids.
244. Multigenerational living is affordable. A second house for Grandma costs what a condo costs in Utah.
245. Community events are genuinely community-scale. You will see your dentist at the parade.
246. Volunteer culture runs deep, from Frontier Days to the fire districts to the fairgrounds.
247. Crime stays at small-city levels, and downtown and the parks are places you'd actually walk at night.
248. Your brother runs a diesel shop here. Nobody in this family waits in line for truck work again.
249. You don't have to give up Utah. Cheyenne is close enough to visit, far enough to breathe, and cheap enough that you can afford both.
250. Laramie County has the extension office, the 4-H program, the vet, and the neighbors who will teach a new family how to run cattle. Nobody starts from zero here.
