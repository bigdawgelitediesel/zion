Why together

Living on the same land is the whole point.

Moving to Wyoming is a good idea on its own. Moving to Wyoming together is a different kind of life. Here is what changes when the people you love are a two-minute walk away instead of a day's drive.

One well, one road, one tractor

Five families buying separately pay for five of everything. Together we pay for a couple of wells, one access road, one power run, one skid steer, one set of fence tools. The savings are not small; the land section below adds it up.

Built-in childcare

Grandma, aunts, uncles, and cousins on the same ground. Sick kid on a workday, date night, a week-long trip: covered, without a daycare bill or a favor you have to pay back.

Parents age in place, surrounded

Nobody has to choose between a care facility and quitting a job. When the folks need help, there are a dozen of us within walking distance. That is what land next to each other buys.

Kids grow up as a tribe

Cousins as daily playmates. Chores that actually mean something: feeding animals, gathering eggs, branding day. They learn to work, to fix things, and to count on family. You cannot buy that in a subdivision.

Food you raised yourself

Beef from your own pasture, eggs from your own coop, vegetables from a garden big enough to matter. One family cannot run a herd. Five families can, and the homestead section shows how much it produces.

Resilient when it counts

Blizzard, power outage, layoff, a hard year. A family on shared land has a generator, a stocked freezer, extra hands, and nobody faces it alone. That is real security, not an insurance policy.

Many hands on the big days

Fencing, haying, branding, pouring a slab, raising a barn. The jobs that break one person are a Saturday with a dozen of us and a grill going afterward.

Holidays without the drive

Thanksgiving is a walk across the pasture. Birthdays, ball games, Sunday dinner: everybody is just here. The years we have left with each other get spent together instead of on I-15.

How we'd hold the land

Option A: one family LLC

A Wyoming LLC (about $100 to form, $60 a year) owns the whole parcel. Each family holds an equal share and has a designated home site. The operating agreement spells out buy-out terms, what happens if someone leaves, and who pays for what. Simplest to buy, simplest to run as a ranch.

Option B: split into parcels

Buy the big piece, then divide it so each family owns its own parcel outright. In Wyoming, divisions of 35 acres or more are generally exempt from subdivision review, so a 160-acre parcel splits into four 40s with minimal red tape. Shared items (road, wells, pasture) go under easements and a small agreement. More independence, more paperwork.

Cattle

Start with a handful of bred cows or pairs, not a herd. Run them on our own pasture through summer, hay them through winter (hay is grown all around Cheyenne), sell the calves in the fall. The herd grows with our fence and our confidence. Laramie County 4-H means the kids can show their own steers at the fair.

Chickens

Thirty hens is a two-coop operation that gives every family a dozen or two a week with plenty to spare. Chickens are the first thing we'd set up and the easiest thing for kids to own. Add meat birds in spring and the freezer fills in eight weeks.

Garden, orchard, and the rest

Cheyenne's season is short and the wind is real, so the garden gets a windbreak, raised beds, and a greenhouse or high tunnel. Potatoes, squash, beans, greens, and root crops do great at 6,000 feet. Add a few pigs on kitchen scraps and a couple of beehives, and the land is feeding us.

    The plan

    How this actually happens.

    Nobody sells a house tomorrow, and nobody buys land with people who aren't here yet. Jacob and Abby are already in Cheyenne. The order is simple: get everyone to town first, then find the land together, then build. There is an off-ramp at every step.

    Phase 1Get everyone to Cheyenne

    1. Step 1

      The family call

      Everyone reads this page. We get on one call and answer the honest questions: who is interested, who is a hard no, who needs more time. No commitments.

    2. Step 2

      Each household picks a move window

      Some can come in six months, some need two years to finish a lease, a school year, or a house sale. All of that is fine. Write the date down anyway.

    3. Step 3

      Jobs lined up before the truck is loaded

      The two of us already here scout openings, make introductions, and know which employers are hiring. Remote workers just need a desk and Wyoming's zero income tax. Anyone in the trades will have offers.

    4. Step 4

      Land in town first

      Rent or buy a regular house in Cheyenne for now. Get the kids in school, learn the town, and build up the down payment with the money Utah was taking. Being here is what lets us look at land in person together.

    Phase 2Find the land together

    1. Step 5

      Decide what we can spend

      Each family runs the land calculator above with real figures. We set a land budget and a per-family share.

    2. Step 6

      Form the Wyoming LLC

      About $100 and an afternoon. Write the operating agreement first: shares, buy-outs, decision rules, what happens if a family needs out. Doing this before we own anything is what keeps us friends.

    3. Step 7

      Land search, all of us, in person

      Target: 80 to 320 acres within 30 minutes of town, year-round road access, power nearby, existing well or good well logs on neighboring parcels, some pasture already fenced. We walk every parcel together on a Saturday.

    4. Step 8

      Due diligence, then buy

      Well logs from the State Engineer, soil and perc tests for septic, a power-extension quote, Laramie County zoning and permit check, title, easements, water rights. If it all checks out, the LLC closes. Land loans on raw acreage usually want 20 to 30 percent down.

    Phase 3Plan the layout, build, and run it

    1. Step 9

      Lay out the compound together

      Home sites, the shared road, where the barn and shop go, where the garden gets its windbreak. Everybody gets a say and a site. The concept map above is the starting sketch.

    2. Step 10

      Road, power, water, septic

      Shared infrastructure goes in first and gets built once. This is where splitting costs pays for itself.

    3. Step 11

      Build in phases

      Whoever is readiest builds first and becomes the on-site project manager. A shop with living quarters is a legitimate first home while houses go up, and the shop is needed anyway.

    4. Step 12

      Fence, barn, chickens, first cattle

      Chickens the first spring. Fence and a barn the first summer. A few bred cows that fall. Small, cheap, and learning as we go.

    5. Step 13

      Everybody home, the place running

      Within a few years every holiday is a walk across the pasture and the land is paying some of its own way.

    Straight talk

    The objections, answered honestly.

    Isn't Cheyenne windy?

    Yes. It is one of the windiest cities in the country. It is also why there are no inversions, why the snow leaves in two days, and why the air is clean in January. You plant a windbreak, you build the garden with a wall, and you stop noticing it by the second year. Everyone who lives here will tell you the same thing.

    What about jobs?

    Cheyenne has a state government, an Air Force base in the middle of a multi-billion-dollar missile modernization, a data center boom (Microsoft, Meta, and more), Union Pacific, two big distribution centers, a regional hospital, and a trades shortage. Denver and Fort Collins are 45 to 90 minutes for anything else, and remote work from Wyoming means zero state income tax.

    I'd miss my friends and my life in Utah.

    Salt Lake is six and a half hours on I-80, same time zone. That is a long weekend any time you want it. And the trade is friends you see monthly for family you see daily.

    What about church?

    Cheyenne has active LDS wards and stakes, and the Fort Collins Temple is 45 minutes away. It also has every other kind of church, and plenty of people who go to none. Nobody here cares which one you pick.

    Living that close, won't we fight?

    Maybe, if we wing it. That is why the LLC operating agreement comes before the land: how decisions get made, what each family pays, how someone exits with their money. Each family also gets its own house, its own yard, and real distance between front doors. Close is not the same as on top of each other.

    None of us know how to raise cattle.

    Our neighbors will. Laramie County has an extension office, a 4-H program, a sale barn, a vet, and ranchers who have been doing this for a century. We start with a few head, we ask questions, and we learn. The chickens and the garden are easy; the cattle come when we are ready.

    What does it cost to build out there?

    Custom homes around Cheyenne run roughly $200 to $275 a square foot in 2026, plus well, septic, and power if the site has none. The land calculator above includes all of it. Building in phases, sharing a contractor, and doing finish work ourselves all bring it down.

    Winter?

    Cheyenne gets about as much snow as Salt Lake and keeps less of it on the ground. The sun and wind clear it. Winters are cold, bright, and dry. You will want a good coat, a plow blade on something, and a generator. You will not want an inversion.

    What if I'm interested but not for years?

    That is fine and expected. The plan is phased. Being in on the land now, with a home site reserved, and moving when the time is right is exactly how this works.
